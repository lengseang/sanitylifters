# Streak & Accountability

A lightweight accountability layer: streaks that actually sting to break, and
small squads that notice when you vanish.

## Problem

Motivation is a spike; consistency is a curve. Apps show you a streak, but nobody
feels it when you break it — so the streak stops mattering after week two.

## Target user

- **The social lifter** who trains better with a group watching.
- **The restarter** who keeps falling off after a few weeks.
- **The friendly competitor** who wants low-stakes peer pressure.

## Why now

Streaks are cheap to build and people already post them. The gap is the *social
consequence* — a squad that gets notified when you miss, and you when they do.

## Core experience

1. Join a squad of 3–5, small enough that absence is obvious.
2. Train to keep your streak alive; the squad sees your check-ins.
3. A miss triggers a nudge and a "what happened?" check-in — recovery, not shame.

## User stories

- As a restarter, I want my squad to notice when I vanish, so someone pulls me back.
- As a social lifter, I want my streak to be visible, so consistency feels shared.
- As a busy member, I want one guilt-free rest day a week, so I don't quit out of shame.

## MVP scope

**In:** streaks, squad of 3–5, miss notifications, a weekly rest-day allowance.

**Out:** money stakes, public leaderboards, full social feed.

## Success metrics

- 30-day retention of squad members vs. solo users.
- Streak survival rate after a squad member checks in.
- Squad activation (messages sent per squad per week).

## Risks

- **Shame backfires** — keep the tone supportive; reward returns, don't punish misses.
- **Notification fatigue** — a nudge is only useful if it's rare and genuine.

## Open questions

- Is the squad self-selected or auto-matched?
- Do we gate streaks behind a real workout, or trust a tap?
